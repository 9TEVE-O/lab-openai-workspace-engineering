# Operating Principles

## 1. Evidence over intentions

A good claim is not enough. Useful AI work needs evidence: source notes, decisions, constraints, review gates, and proof that the workflow behaves as described.

## 2. Skills before sprawl

When the same kind of AI work repeats, convert it into a reusable skill instead of letting it live as another loose prompt.

## 3. Source-locked where possible

When a claim depends on a source, keep the source visible. If the source is missing, label the statement as an assumption, inference, or open question.

## 4. Build narrow before broad

A controlled workflow that solves one real problem is more valuable than a broad AI system that cannot be tested.

## 5. Approval before automation

For sensitive workflows, AI may assist, classify, extract, and draft, but human approval must control final ingestion, publishing, sending, exporting, and client-facing use.

## 6. Reuse the spine

Every project should answer:

- What is the problem?
- Who validates the output?
- What evidence proves it worked?
- What is blocked until review?
- What reusable skill or checklist does this create?
